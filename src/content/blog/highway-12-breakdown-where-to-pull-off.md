Highway 12 is the road our drivers talk about most. It runs the width of the county in two
very different halves: west through Jameson Canyon between the Cordelia Junction and Napa,
and east from Suisun City out across the delta to the Rio Vista bridge. Both halves share
the same problem. When something goes wrong there, the places to put a car are few and far
apart, and most drivers do not know where the next one is.

![Where to pull off on Highway 12 between Suisun City and Rio Vista: the Grizzly Island Road junction, Birds Landing Road, Shiloh Road, Collinsville Road, Church Road and the Highway 113 junction at Rio Vista, with long stretches of levee and farmland between them where the shoulder narrows to almost nothing](/img/highway-12-pull-offs.svg)

## Why this road is unforgiving

Through the delta, Highway 12 is mostly two lanes with a narrow shoulder, and in the levee
sections there is barely any shoulder at all, with a drop and water or a drainage ditch
immediately beyond it. Traffic moves at highway speed, there are farm vehicles and heavy
trucks in the mix, and the gaps between anything resembling a business run for miles.

Add the local weather. Wind comes across the Montezuma Hills hard enough to run a wind
farm, which is lively for anything high sided. Winter brings tule fog that can close
visibility down to a car length. Summer afternoons run hot with no shade anywhere.

Jameson Canyon, on the west end, is a faster four lane run now, but the shoulder through
the canyon is thin and the traffic volume between Solano and Napa counties is heavy all
day.

## Where you can actually get off

Heading east from Suisun City toward Rio Vista, these are the places with real room,
roughly in order:

- **The Grizzly Island Road junction.** The last easy pull off before the long open run,
  with room to get a car well clear of the lane.
- **Birds Landing Road.** A proper intersection out in the hills with space on the corner.
- **Shiloh Road and Collinsville Road.** Side roads south toward the river, both wide
  enough at the junction to be out of the traffic stream.
- **Church Road and the Highway 113 junction** at the Rio Vista end, where you are close
  to town and to services.
- **Rio Vista itself.** Once you are across the Highway 113 junction you are into town,
  with lots, fuel and somewhere to wait indoors.

Going west toward Napa, the honest list is shorter: get back to the Cordelia Junction and
the truck stops on Central Way, or continue through to the Napa County line. In the canyon
itself, take any wide spot you can find and stay in the car.

If the car is still moving at all, reaching one of these beats stopping where you are. A
limping car that makes it to a junction is a safe, ordinary tow. A car stopped on a levee
shoulder is a hazard for everyone, yourself included.

## Stay in the vehicle

On this road more than any other we cover, stay belted inside with the hazards on.

The temptation is to get out and stand clear, which is the right instinct on a wide
freeway shoulder with a barrier. Out here there is nowhere to stand. The ground beyond the
pavement is often soft, sloped, or straight into a ditch, and oncoming drivers coming out
of a bend or out of fog have very little time to register a person. Your car is a large
lit object. You are not.

The exception stays the same everywhere: smoke or fire means get out and get well away.

## How to be found

Addresses do not exist out there, so give the dispatcher something a driver can navigate
to:

- **Direction of travel.** Eastbound toward Rio Vista, or westbound toward Suisun.
- **The last cross road you passed,** by name. This is the single most useful detail.
- **A mile marker** if you can see one of the small green paddles at the edge of the
  pavement.
- **What you can see.** The wind turbines, a bridge, a farm building, the marsh, a sign for
  a side road ahead. Out there a landmark genuinely narrows it down.
- **Which side of the road you are on,** since there is no turning around on a levee.

Dropping a pin from your phone and sending it is faster than any description, and worth
doing before your battery gets low.

## Night, fog and the wind

After dark the delta stretch has no street lighting of any kind. If you are stopped, leave
the hazards going and the parking lights on, and if you have a reflective triangle in the
trunk, set it well back up the road only if there is a safe place to walk to, which on a
levee usually means there is not.

In fog, do not stop on the pavement if there is any way to avoid it. A stationary car in
dense tule fog is the classic setup for a chain collision, which is why the advice on
[driving in tule fog](/blog/tule-fog-i80-solano-county/) is to leave the road entirely
rather than stop on a shoulder. If you have no choice, hazards on, seatbelts on, and call
immediately so the responders know you are there.

In high wind, be aware that opening a door against the gusts out there can take it out of
your hand, and a trailer parked on a soft shoulder can shift.
