The Cordelia Junction, where I-80, I-680, and Highway 12 come together on the west edge of Fairfield, strands more drivers than any other stretch of road in Solano County. Merging traffic from three directions, sudden slowdowns near the truck scales, and strong wind off the Suisun Marsh all pile up in one interchange. If your car just died there, here's the local playbook.

## 1. Get as far right as you can, then stop moving

If the car still rolls, aim for the shoulder past the gore point (the striped triangle where lanes split), not inside it. The exits at Green Valley Road, Suisun Valley Road, and Central Way all have wide shoulders and are safer than the interchange ramps themselves. If you can limp to the Central Way truck stop area, even better: parking lots beat shoulders every time.

## 2. Hazards on, stay belted, stay inside

On this interchange, the shoulder is not a safe place to stand. Keep your seatbelt on and stay in the car unless you can exit on the passenger side and get behind a guardrail, well away from traffic. Freeway shoulders are where tow operators get hurt, and they wear reflective gear for a reason you don't have.

## 3. Know your location before you call

The single biggest delay in junction tows is location confusion, because "I'm at the 80/680 split" describes four different places. Before you call anyone, note:

- **Direction of travel** (I-80 westbound toward Vallejo, eastbound toward Vacaville, I-680 southbound, Hwy 12 toward Suisun or toward Napa)
- **The last exit you passed** (Green Valley Rd, Suisun Valley Rd, Central Way, Abernathy, Red Top Rd)
- **Which side of the freeway you're on**

Those three details routinely save ten minutes of a truck circling the interchange.

## 4. Call for a truck, and ask two questions

Whoever you call, ask for a firm price (hook fee plus mileage, as one number) and a realistic ETA. If either answer is vague, call someone else. Local dispatch reaches most junction calls in about 20 to 30 minutes because trucks stage near Cordelia; anything quoting over an hour is coming from out of the area.

## 5. If CHP arrives first

The California Highway Patrol patrols this interchange heavily. If an officer stops, they may offer to call a rotation tow to clear the road quickly. You can accept, but know that rotation tows usually deliver to a storage yard with daily fees, not to the shop of your choice. If your car is safely on the shoulder and not blocking traffic, you're generally free to wait for the tow you called.

## What it costs

Local tows from the junction into Fairfield, Suisun City, or Vacaville are a hook fee plus a few miles, quoted before dispatch. Longer hauls toward the Bay Area or Sacramento are priced per mile. There's no legitimate reason for the price to change between the phone call and the drop-off.
