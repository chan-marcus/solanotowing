## Check the easy things first

Before anyone drives out to you, spend two minutes on the free options.

**Walk the whole car.** The driver's door locking does not always mean the rest did. Try
every door, the tailgate or trunk, and on a hatchback the rear glass if it opens
separately.

**Check your phone.** If your car is from roughly the last decade and you ever set up the
manufacturer's app, there is a good chance it can unlock the doors from your phone. Most
brands offer this, and it costs nothing. It is worth the ninety seconds it takes to log
in.

**Think about the spare.** Not where it should be, but where it actually is. A partner ten
minutes away with the spare key beats any service call.

## One situation that is not a lockout call

If a child or a pet is locked inside, call 911 first. Do not wait for a tow truck.

A closed car on a warm Fairfield afternoon heats up far faster than people expect, and the
inside of a car parked in the sun can reach dangerous temperatures in minutes, not hours.
The fire department will open it, and if the fastest way in is a window, that is the right
trade and nobody will second guess it. The same goes for a car locked with the engine
running in an enclosed garage.

Tell the dispatcher exactly who is inside and how long they have been there. That changes
how the call is prioritized.

## Why the coat hanger is a bad idea

Everyone has seen it done in a film, and on a car from 1985 it often worked. Modern doors
are a different object.

Inside the door skin of a current car are the window regulator and its motor, the lock
actuator and its linkage, wiring for the mirror, speakers and switches, and on many
vehicles the side curtain airbag and its sensor. A wire fished blindly past the weather
seal catches all of it. The repair bill for a torn regulator or a damaged airbag circuit
is many times the cost of the lockout, and it is not covered by anything.

The weather seal itself is the other casualty. Once it is creased or pulled loose, the door
whistles at freeway speed and leaks in the rain.

Breaking glass is also worse than it sounds. The small fixed quarter window is not cheap,
side glass showers the interior with fragments that stay in the seat rails for years, and
on many cars the window is laminated and simply does not break the way people expect.

## What a lockout actually involves

The tool kit is an air wedge and a long reach tool. The wedge goes into the top corner of
the door frame and creates a gap of a few millimeters, wide enough to pass a coated rod
through without touching the glass or the seal. From there the technician works the
interior handle, the lock button, or on a keyless car the unlock switch on the door panel.

On most vehicles it takes a few minutes. Some take longer: deadlocking systems, frameless
windows on coupes and convertibles, and a few European models need a different approach,
and a good operator will tell you on the phone if yours is one of them.

Expect to be asked for proof the car is yours. A license that matches the registration, or
a registration document in your name once the door is open, is normal and it protects you.

## When a lockout will not fix it

Worth being straight about the cases where opening the door is not the answer:

- **The key is lost, not locked inside.** Getting the door open does not start the car. A
  modern key has to be cut and programmed to the vehicle, which is a locksmith or dealer
  job. What we can do is [tow the car](/services/emergency-towing/) to wherever that work
  is happening.
- **The key is locked in the trunk** of a car with no pass-through and no trunk release
  inside the cabin.
- **The key broke off in the lock or the ignition.** Same answer: that is key work, not
  door work.
- **The key fob battery died.** Many fobs hide a mechanical blade key inside, and most cars
  have a hidden keyhole on the driver's door, often behind a small cover on the handle.
  Your owner's manual shows both. That one you can usually solve in the parking lot.

## What to tell dispatch

Four things get the right person to you quickly:

- **Year, make and model.** This decides which tools come out of the truck.
- **Exactly where you are.** In a garage, name the level and the nearest stairwell. In a
  big lot, name the store or the row. "The Premium Outlets" is a very large area.
- **Whether the engine is running** and whether anyone is inside.
- **Whether you can show the car is yours.** If your wallet is also locked in the car, say
  so up front rather than at the end.

## What it costs

A lockout is a flat call-out fee, quoted on the phone before anyone is dispatched, and it
does not change at 2am. There is no mileage component the way there is on a tow, because
nothing gets towed. If the car turns out to need key work rather than door work, you will
be told that on the phone when you describe the problem, not after a truck has arrived.
