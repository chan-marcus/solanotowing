Not every breakdown needs a tow truck. A good share of the calls we run in

## What gets fixed where you're parked

**Flat tire.** If you have a serviceable spare, an operator swaps it on the spot.
This is the one people most often try themselves and most often shouldn't. More on that below.

**Dead battery.** A professional jump pack, plus a check that the alternator is
actually charging before the truck leaves. That check matters: a jump on a failed
alternator just strands you again a few miles later.

**Out of fuel.** Enough gasoline or diesel to reach the nearest station. It
happens more than you'd think on the stretch between the Cordelia Junction and
the Central Way pumps, where drivers gamble on one more exit.

**Keys locked in.** Wedge-and-reach tools open the car without drilling the lock
or breaking glass. If a child or pet is inside, say so immediately, but call 911 first, because Fairfield PD and Suisun Fire will break a window when seconds
matter, and that is the right call.

**A loose or corroded battery terminal.** Unglamorous, and it perfectly imitates
a dead battery. Sometimes it's a two-minute fix.

## What genuinely needs the truck

- **No spare, or a shredded tire.** Many newer cars ship with a sealant kit
  instead of a spare, and sealant does nothing for a sidewall tear.
- **Won't stay running after a jump.** That's the alternator or something
  deeper. The car needs a shop, not another boost.
- **Overheating or a coolant leak.** Especially on the Vaca grade in summer.
  Driving an overheated engine "just to the next exit" is how a $40 hose becomes
  a head gasket.
- **Collision damage.** If it won't roll or steer, it rides on a flatbed.
- **Off the pavement.** A wheel in the ditch on Suisun Valley Road after rain
  needs a winch, not a push.

## The freeway shoulder is not a driveway

This is the part worth reading twice. Changing a tire in your own driveway is a
chore; doing it on the I-80 shoulder near Air Base Parkway or the Cordelia scales
is genuinely dangerous. Traffic is moving at 70 mph a few feet from your
shoulder, and the passing-side wheels are the ones you'd be kneeling beside.

If you're stopped on the freeway:

1. Get as far right as the car will roll, past the gore point, not inside it.
2. Hazards on, and keep them on.
3. **Stay in the car with your seatbelt fastened** unless you can get out the
   passenger side and stand behind a barrier, well away from traffic.
4. Give dispatch your **direction of travel** and the **last exit you passed**.
   On the I-80/I-680/Highway 12 interchange, "I'm at the 80/680 split" describes
   four different places, and that ambiguity costs ten minutes.

Operators do this work with the truck positioned as a shield and amber warning
lights running. That's the actual product you're buying on a freeway call, not just the tire.

## Roadside assistance vs. your auto club

Plenty of drivers here have coverage through AAA, an insurer, or a card benefit,
and if it's included you should use it. Two honest caveats.

The first is wait times. Club dispatch routes to whichever contractor is
available, which on a holiday weekend or during a fog-related pileup can mean an
hour or more. A local operator already staged near the Junction is often closer.

The second is what's covered. Many plans cap the tow at a set mileage and bill
you for the rest, and some limit how many calls you get per year. If you're
outside those limits, calling direct is frequently cheaper than the overage.

## What it costs

Roadside calls are a **flat call-out fee**, quoted before dispatch, and it's the
same rate at 3am as at 3pm. If you're quoted a big after-hours surcharge, keep
calling.

If the job turns out to need a tow after all, the operator already on scene escalates: you get the tow price up front, and you don't go back to the end of
the queue. One call covers both the attempt and the fallback.

## Before you dial

Have these ready and the whole thing goes faster:

- **Where you are:** cross street, parking row, or direction plus last exit
- **What you drive:** year, make, model, and mention AWD or an EV
- **What happened:** "flat rear passenger, no spare" beats "car trouble"
- **Where it's going**, if it needs a tow: shop, home, or dealer
